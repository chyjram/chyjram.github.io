I am a Postdoctoral Research Fellow in the Physical Biology of Gene Expression (PBGX) Lab at Boise State University (March 2025 – January 2026), working with Dr. Matthew L. Research

Areas of Investigation

Combining microsecond molecular dynamics, enhanced sampling, and quantum chemistry to understand how carbohydrate rings move — and why it matters biologically.

⬡

Pyranose Ring Puckering Dynamics

Characterising the full conformational landscape of hexopyranose rings — chair, half-chair, envelope, boat, skew-boat — and free energy barriers between them using microsecond MD and enhanced sampling methods.

⚡

Polarizable Force Field Development

Reparametrising and refining the CHARMM Drude polarizable force field for hexose monosaccharides and their carboxylate and N-acetyl amine derivatives, benchmarked against NMR, XRD, and QM data.

◎

Enhanced Sampling Methods

Applying eABF and well-tempered metadynamics to overcome high free energy barriers in pyranose puckering transitions inaccessible to standard MD; QM investigations of transition state geometries.

⬡

Spatiotemporal Genome Dynamics

Postdoctoral work at Boise State: 3D orbital tracking of RNA regulatory kinetics in living cells, stochastic Gillespie simulations, and coarse-grained modelling to characterise gene expression dynamics.

Impact of Polarization on the Ring Puckering Dynamics of Hexose Monosaccharides

Crystal structure analysis of ten aldohexose anomers reveals multiple ring conformations. Microsecond MD and eABF enhanced sampling with the CHARMM36 additive and Drude polarizable force fields show that polarization substantially lowers the ⁴C₁–¹C₄ energy barrier and enhances conformational diversity, while the additive field is overly rigid — motivating reparametrisation of Drude parameters.

Drude Polarizable FF eABF Microsecond MD JCIM 2023
Probing Conformational Dynamics of Hexose Monosaccharides Using Transition States: A Quantum Mechanical Investigation

Well-tempered metadynamics generates transition paths between the ⁴C₁ and ¹C₄ conformers of major aldohexoses, followed by QM calculations in gas-phase and explicit water. The results evaluate enzymatic ring-distortion hypotheses and assess conformational energy landscapes from first principles.
